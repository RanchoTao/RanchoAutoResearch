# Paper implications

## Safe claim

At matched KL/NLL damage, block and noise interventions differ systematically
in logit perturbation norm and boundary-axis alignment. A small preregistered
output-geometry block explains about 20% of the corrected aggregate family
coefficient, and the residual is absent in the high-margin stratum. However,
anchored boundary displacement alone explains none of the aggregate effect,
and substantial family-specific structure remains.

## Unsupported claims

- Decision-boundary geometry causes Candidate A.
- Boundary displacement explains the aggregate residual.
- Geometry explains the 24 corrected sign reversals.
- Geometry explains layer heterogeneity.
- Residual magnitude increases monotonically toward the decision boundary.
- Logit norm or alignment is individually responsible; they are strongly
  correlated.

## Reviewer attack

An ICLR reviewer would first note that the partial 20.18% M3 shrinkage is just
above an arbitrary threshold, fails equivalence, and is contradicted by the
geometry-matched and layer analyses. Output geometry may be a correlate of an
unobserved internal intervention direction rather than a mechanism.

The current result is useful as a disciplined narrowing: a paper-level account
must identify intervention-family structure inside the network, not add more
logits-derived summaries.
