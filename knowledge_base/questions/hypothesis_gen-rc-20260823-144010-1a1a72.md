# Stage 08: Hypothesis Gen

The strongest proposal is a staged program: first verify that “observation-process shift” is a real phenomenon rather than a severity artifact; then test conditional transition invariance as the primary method; retain erasure-coded memory as the high-risk alternative. A matched model-free comparison determines whether this is genuinely a world-model problem.

1. Higher-order missingness structure matters beyond information loss

Claim:
Independent dropout, burst dropout, and delay remain meaningfully different after matching their visible-frame rate, observation-age distribution, and maximum missing-run length.

Rationale:
This is the essential gatekeeper. Without severity matching, apparent process-shift failures may simply reflect longer open-loop prediction. Conversely, if performance differences persist after matching, the case for explicitly modeling observation structure becomes substantially stronger.

Method:
Generate matched corruptions of identical trajectories. Fit the same RSSM under each training process and evaluate all train-test combinations. Include a simple mask-plus-observation-age baseline. Use process identity only for analysis, not model training.

Measurable prediction:

- Matched corruption families should still differ by at least 10 percentage points in normalized return.
- Adding corruption identity to a regression already containing visible fraction, observation age, and maximum gap should increase explained return variance by at least 0.10.
- A mask-plus-age baseline should remain at least 8 points below the best process-aware model on one or more held-out processes.

Failure condition:
Reject the structural-shift hypothesis if matched processes differ by less than 5 points, process identity adds less than 0.05 explained variance, and mask-plus-age conditioning closes the performance gap within 5 points.

Feasibility:
No new architecture is required. The experiment reuses trajectories with offline corruption and should take approximately 3–5 GPU-hours for three seeds and several small RSSMs.

Unresolved disagreement:
The innovator assumes sensor mechanisms create meaningful structural transformations. The contrarian argues that most of the effect is merely observation age and gap length. This experiment must be completed before interpreting any architectural gain as observation-process generalization.


2. Conditional transition invariance improves transfer without discarding informative missingness

Claim:
Aligning action-conditioned latent transition predictions across paired corruptions will improve transfer under state-independent missingness, but only if masks and observation age remain available to the posterior and policy. Universal mask invariance should fail when missingness is state-dependent.

Rationale:
This preserves the innovator’s strongest idea—sensor changes as alternative views of shared dynamics—while accepting the contrarian’s identifiability objection. The model should make environment transitions invariant across sensor views without forcing posterior beliefs to be identical or pretending that masks never carry state information.

Method:
Use a small RSSM with:

- a shared transition prior;
- a mask- and age-conditioned posterior gate;
- an auxiliary consistency loss between one- to five-step transition priors from two corrupted versions of the same trajectory.

Test separately on:

- MCAR corruption independent of state;
- MNAR corruption triggered by position, action, or velocity.

Compare against ordinary RSSM, mask concatenation, mixed-corruption augmentation, posterior-state matching, a larger capacity-matched RSSM, and transition consistency without a mask pathway.

Measurable prediction:

- Under held-out MCAR corruption, conditional transition consistency should improve normalized return by at least 8 points over equal-data augmentation.
- It should reduce five-step reward-prediction error by at least 15%.
- Under MNAR corruption, retaining the mask pathway should outperform unconditional invariance by at least 8 return points.
- A mask-only probe should reach R² ≥ 0.30 under MNAR but remain below 0.10 under MCAR.

Failure condition:
Reject the hypothesis if equal-data augmentation or mask concatenation matches the method within 3 points, if transition consistency improves return by less than 5 points, or if the conditional design fails to prevent the predicted MNAR degradation.

Feasibility:
Paired corruptions reuse the same trajectories and require only a second encoder pass plus a small gating MLP. Expected cost is 30–45 minutes per seed on one 8 GB GPU, or approximately 6–9 GPU-hours for the central comparisons.

Unresolved disagreement:
The innovator expects an invariant latent transition to be the key transferable object. The contrarian argues that transition and observation dynamics may be non-identifiable and legitimately entangled. The MCAR/MNAR split makes that disagreement directly falsifiable.
