# SMART Research Goal

## **Topic**

World models for model-based reinforcement learning under structured partial observability, focusing on whether latent dynamics incorrectly encode the observation schedule rather than the underlying environment state.

## **Novel Angle**

### Leading gap: observation-schedule shortcut learning

The proposed failure mode is not generic robustness to missing frames. It is **observation-process entanglement**: when missing observations follow a predictable structure during training, a recurrent state-space model may use the dropout phase, visibility pattern, or time since last observation as a shortcut for predicting state. The resulting latent dynamics can work under the training schedule yet fail when the environment dynamics remain unchanged and only the observation schedule changes.

This is a timely question because modern world models increasingly rely on compact recurrent latent states for prediction and control, while their evaluation usually assumes a fixed observation pipeline. Existing strategies such as recurrent memory, frame stacking, masking augmentation, and reconstruction losses may handle in-distribution missingness without ensuring that environment state is invariant to the observation process.

The proposed mechanism is a **visibility-isolated latent update**:

- The action-conditioned dynamics prior is prevented from receiving visibility masks or observation age.
- Mask information is used only to gate posterior evidence into the prior.
- Optionally, a lightweight consistency loss aligns environment latents obtained from two differently masked versions of the same trajectory.

This differs from standard missing-frame augmentation because it imposes a structural separation between **environment dynamics** and **observation availability**, then evaluates that separation under explicit train–test changes in the observation process.

Novelty remains provisional until the literature audit establishes that prior work has not already combined:

1. structured observation-schedule shifts;
2. explicit visibility isolation in latent dynamics; and
3. downstream planning or control evaluation.

If a close prior method already makes and tests this claim, the project should pivot or be killed rather than renamed.

### Ranked candidate questions

#### 1. Observation-schedule shortcut learning — selected

- **Setting:** Pixel-based continuous control with actions and an observation-availability indicator.
- **Partial observability:** Training frames are missing according to a periodic schedule.
- **Task:** Latent prediction and control.
- **Expected failure:** A standard recurrent world model entangles periodic mask phase with physical state and degrades when dropout period or phase changes.
- **Hypothesis:** Visibility-isolated posterior fusion preserves environment-state information and improves control under unseen schedules.
- **Falsifier:** Ordinary dropout augmentation matches the proposed method, or the standard model shows no meaningful schedule-shift degradation.
- **Broader importance:** Real sensors change sampling rates, outage durations, and synchronization without changing the controlled system.

#### 2. Reacquisition shock after contiguous dropout

- **Setting:** The agent receives no image for several consecutive steps and then regains visibility.
- **Expected failure:** The first returning observation causes an excessive posterior jump, destroying useful predicted state.
- **Hypothesis:** An observation-age-conditioned evidence gate reduces damaging latent discontinuities.
- **Falsifier:** Posterior jumps do not correlate with control failures, or simple gradient clipping and dropout augmentation perform equally well.
- **Broader importance:** Relevant to intermittent sensing, communications outages, and asynchronous perception.

#### 3. Moving spatial-occluder shortcut

- **Setting:** A deterministic occluder traverses pixel observations during training but follows unseen trajectories at test time.
- **Expected failure:** The model treats occluder motion as part of physical dynamics.
