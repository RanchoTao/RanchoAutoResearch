# Stage 07: Synthesis

# Cluster Overview

The literature supports four connected research clusters:

1. **General latent world models for prediction and control** establish that compact recurrent state-space models can support planning or policy learning from pixels, but rarely isolate partial observability as an experimental variable.
2. **Belief and history representations for POMDPs** show that useful sufficient statistics can be explicitly learned or emerge implicitly, substantially narrowing the novelty available for generic “add memory” or “learn a belief state” proposals.
3. **Mechanisms for particular forms of partial observability** address delayed observations and selective temporal memory, but do not yet establish broad robustness to changes in the observation-generating process.
4. **Structured and robust representations** study contrastive, object-centric, physics-constrained, and systematically generalizing latent models. These works challenge simple representation-learning claims while leaving the separation of environment dynamics from a changing observation process comparatively underexplored.

The most credible opening is therefore not “world models under partial observability” broadly. It is the narrower question of whether a world model trained under one structured observation process can preserve decision-relevant latent dynamics when the observation process changes at test time.

# Cluster 1: Latent World Models for Imagination and Control

Foundational world-model research demonstrates that compressed latent dynamics can support policies trained in imagined trajectories and transferred to real environments (ha2018world). PlaNet introduced deterministic and stochastic latent dynamics with multi-step training for planning from pixels, including tasks containing partial observability (hafner2018latent). Dreamer and its successors showed that policies can be optimized through latent imagination at increasing scale and across diverse domains (hafner2019dream; hafner2020mastering; hafner2023mastering).

Collectively, these papers establish:

- recurrent latent models are viable substrates for history integration;
- multi-step latent prediction can support planning and control;
- stochastic latent states can represent uncertainty not visible in a single frame;
- imagined trajectories can produce effective policies.

However, partial observability is generally embedded in task design rather than systematically manipulated. The supplied evidence does not show controlled comparisons across occlusion, missing frames, delay, sensor dropout, or train-test changes in visibility. Consequently, these works establish the baseline capability but not robustness to structured observation-process shift.

The model-based RL survey explicitly identifies partial observability as a central model-learning problem, while offering no focused empirical resolution of structured observation failure modes (moerland2023modelbased).

# Cluster 2: Belief States, Memory, and Predictive Representations

Classical POMDP work establishes that control may require an internal statistic of action-observation history rather than the current observation alone (kaelbling1998planning; aberdeen2003policygradient). Predictive state representations provide an alternative in which state is grounded in predictions about future action-observation sequences (izadi2003planning).

Recent work sharpens this connection:

- The Wasserstein Believer jointly learns a latent POMDP and approximate belief updater, with guarantees connecting belief approximation to value learning (avalos2023wasserstein).
- Recurrent value networks empirically encode posterior information about control-relevant hidden variables while discarding irrelevant information (lambrechts2022recurrent).
- Belief-like representations can emerge from value learning without explicit belief supervision when recurrent capacity is sufficient (hennig2023emergence).
- Privileged-state pretraining can produce uncertainty-aware beliefs that improve decentralized multi-agent control (pritz2025belief).
- Memory-based architectures improve zero-shot RL under partial observability relative to memory-free alternatives (jeen2025zeroshot).

This cluster creates a strong novelty barrier. The following claims are already substantially covered:

- recurrence can summarize partially observed histories;
- hidden states can become belief-like;
- explicit belief objectives can improve decision making;
- uncertainty-aware belief updates can be learned;
- memory improves control under partial observability.

A new project must therefore specify what ordinary recurrent inference or an existing learned belief updater fails to recover. A credible distinction would be failure under a train-test change in the observation process, especially when environment dynamics remain unchanged.
