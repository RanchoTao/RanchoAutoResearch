# Stage 01: Topic Init

# SMART Research Goal

## Topic

Claim–evidence verification for autonomous AI research agents: determining whether a scientific claim is justified by its literature context, experimental design, executable artifacts, and observed results.

## Novel Angle

The proposed contribution is **intervention-consistent verification of complete research bundles**.

Most established evaluations isolate only one part of the problem: textual scientific fact-checking, citation entailment, paper reviewing, code reproducibility, or generic LLM judging. Static classification also permits shallow shortcuts: a verifier can predict the correct support label without identifying which evidence actually determines that label.

This project will instead evaluate whether a verifier responds correctly to **controlled, scientifically meaningful interventions** on the same research bundle. Each valid bundle will be paired with variants that alter one decisive factor, such as:

- Removing the strongest baseline.
- Reducing the number of random seeds.
- Introducing train/test leakage.
- Hiding a contradictory result.
- Expanding a claim beyond the evaluated environments.
- Converting correlation into a causal assertion.
- Changing the compute budget or evaluation metric.
- Swapping the claimed nearest-neighbor work.
- Creating disagreement between the report and executable artifacts.

The verifier must predict support status, diagnose the induced failure, and localize the evidence responsible for its judgment. Its decision should change when—and only when—the intervention changes what the evidence warrants.

This is timely because autonomous research systems increasingly generate literature reviews, experiments, code, and claims as an integrated workflow, while LLM-as-judge research has exposed calibration, bias, and self-consistency limitations. Existing static fact-checking and review benchmarks do not appear to establish performance on counterfactually paired, artifact-aware research traces. That precise claim must be confirmed or rejected during the required 2023–2026 literature audit.

The direction differs from standard approaches in three ways:

1. It verifies a complete research trace rather than an isolated claim–abstract pair.
2. It measures causal sensitivity to evidence interventions rather than only label accuracy.
3. It prioritizes dangerous false support and actionable failure localization over general review quality.

The methodological hypothesis is that a compact **evidence graph plus adversarial falsification pass** will reduce false-support errors and improve intervention consistency relative to generic and checklist-based LLM judges.

## Scope

Produce one focused benchmark-and-method paper covering:

- Machine-learning research claims only.
- Approximately 150–300 traceable claim–evidence bundles.
- A mixture of genuine supported cases and controlled semi-synthetic mutations.
- Five primary support states: supported, partially supported, unsupported, contradicted, and insufficient/unverifiable.
- Eight to ten failure families drawn from claim inflation, leakage, missing baselines, confounding, weak statistics, selective evidence, unsupported generalization, compute unfairness, and artifact/report mismatch.
- One proposed verifier: evidence-graph construction followed by targeted falsification questions and risk-sensitive aggregation.
- Four necessary comparison families, with matched evidence and model-call budgets wherever possible.

The paper will not attempt universal scientific truth verification, broad automated peer review, fully autonomous experimentation, or expensive model training.

## Benchmark

**Name:** Autonomous Research Claim Verification Benchmark (ARCV-Bench), provisional new benchmark.

**Source:**

- Public machine-learning papers and supplements.
- Associated public code, configurations, logs, and result tables where available.
- Public review material or research-agent traces where licensing permits.
- Scientifically controlled mutations applied to traceable source bundles.
- SciFact as an external textual claim-verification transfer set, not as a substitute for artifact-aware evaluation.

**Unit of evaluation:**  
`(claim, literature context, experiment design, artifacts, results) → support status, failure types, decisive evidence, confidence`

**Splitting protocol:**

- Group by source paper to prevent near-duplicate leakage.
- Hold out mutation families or scientific scenarios at test time.
- Balance lexical cues across valid and invalid pairs.
- Preserve paired originals and mutations for intervention analysis.
- Record source and transformation provenance for every case.

- Primary: risk-weighted error assigning the highest cost to false support.
- False-support rate.
- False-rejection rate.
- Macro-F1 over support statuses.
- Macro-F1 and per-type recall for failure diagnosis.
- Evidence-localization precision, recall, and F1.
