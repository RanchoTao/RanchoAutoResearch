009                                               A BSTRACT
010
011            Interventions on neural-network components are often interpreted as measuring
012            properties such as redundancy or robustness, yet recent work shows that the con-
013            clusion can depend on the intervention protocol. We test whether such protocol
014            dependence can be reduced to differences in output-level functional damage. Our
015            assay measures the fraction of intact next-token top-1 predictions preserved after an
016            interior-block intervention across independently pretrained small P YTHIA models.
017            The early-to-late block-bypass response replicates across nine 160M runs, a held-
018
               out run, a 70M scale, and WikiText-2 and HellaSwag-derived evaluation streams.
               Within residual attenuation, matched comparisons show that KL/NLL predictive
019
               damage discriminates top-1 damage more strongly than raw activation displace-
020
               ment. However, prospective joint KL/NLL matching does not make block bypass
021            and norm-controlled activation noise interchangeable: a corrected family residual
022            of −0.0168 remains across five runs. A frozen block of simple output-logit and
023            decision-boundary features accounts for only 20.2% of that residual. These results
024            support a bounded methodological conclusion: in the tested setting, intervention
025            responses are conditioned on how a model is perturbed and cannot be summarized
026            by raw magnitude or matched predictive damage alone. We do not identify a causal
027            mechanism, establish downstream capability loss, or claim generality beyond the
028            tested small-model regime.

      1    I NTRODUCTION
032
      Interventions—removing a layer, patching an activation, or injecting noise—are widely used to
033
      infer redundancy, necessity, robustness, or causal roles in neural networks. Such interpretations are
034
      strongest when reasonable intervention procedures agree and weaker when the response depends on
035   the measurement protocol.
036
037   This concern is established empirically. Language models can preserve many next-token predictions
038
      after layer deletion or swapping (Lad et al., 2025), while Garcia (2026) shows that output-grounded
      layer equivalence changes across training and differs between replacement and interchange. Interven-
039
      tion audits also show dependence on the comparator and matching procedure (Luo et al., 2026). We
040
      therefore ask a narrower question: can protocol-dependent responses be explained by differences in
041   functional predictive damage?
042
043   The explanation is plausible. Nominally equal interventions can change output distributions differ-
044
      ently, while interventions far apart in activation space can be close in function space. If matching
      forward KL and target-token NLL removes protocol dependence, intervention identity adds little
045
      beyond a severity budget. If not, raw size and those output-level measures are insufficient summaries.
046
047   We study independently pretrained 70M- and 160M-parameter P YTHIA models (Biderman et al.,
048   2023). For a fixed corpus and checkpoint, we intervene on an interior block and measure the retained
049   fraction S of intact next-token top-1 predictions; DS = 1−S is top-1 damage and ∆S = Slate −Searly
050
      is the training endpoint change. This operational metric is not new: it belongs to the relative-accuracy
      family (Lad et al., 2025) and complements the divergent-token rate on identical positions (Deiseroth
051
      et al., 2024).
052
053   The block-bypass endpoint is negative in all nine 160M runs, including a prospectively held-out run,
      and repeats at 70M and on a HellaSwag-derived stream. This establishes a stable outcome, not novelty

      for the broad training phenomenon. Within residual attenuation, complementary matching shows
055   that KL/NLL damage is more informative than raw activation displacement, without establishing
056   KL/NLL causality.

058
      The central test prospectively targets activation-noise strengths, using only KL and NLL, to match
      block interventions by run, checkpoint, and layer. After a tie-handling inconsistency was withdrawn
059

060

061   on average. A frozen output-logit and decision-boundary feature block reduces this residual by 20.2%
062   but does not eliminate it.
063
064
      Our contributions are exactly three:
065
            1. We establish an independently replicated empirical base for a fixed block-bypass response
066
               across two small P YTHIA scales and two English evaluation streams, including held-out,
067
               confidence, and layer controls.
068
069         2. Complementary magnitude- and damage-matched contrasts show that output-level predictive
070            damage is more informative than raw activation displacement within the frozen residual-
071            attenuation assay.
072         3. Prospective joint KL/NLL targeting and a corrected deterministic top-1 analysis show that
073            predictive-damage matching does not eliminate the block/noise difference; simple output
074            geometry explains only part of it.
075
076   We do not infer universal specialization, large-model scaling, downstream capability loss, KL/NLL
077   causality, or an identified internal mechanism.

      2   R ELATED W ORK
081
082   Layer redundancy and protocol dependence. Layer deletion and swapping can preserve substan-
083
      tial next-token behavior (Lad et al., 2025); depth-pruning criteria likewise remove some final-model
      layers with limited aggregate degradation (Men et al., 2025). Our closest neighbor is Garcia (2026),
084
      who shows that replacement and interchange yield different output-grounded gaps across Pythia
085
      checkpoints. That work occupies the broad protocol- and training-dependence claim. We ask
086   whether two intervention families become interchangeable after matching output KL and NLL across
087   independent pretraining runs.
088
089
      Training-dependent perturbation fragility. Quantization degradation varies across pretraining
090
      and learning-rate phases (Catalan-Tatjer et al., 2026); activation-noise fragility can change after probe
091   accuracy saturates (Reblitz-Richardson, 2026), and layer-specific noise reveals heterogeneous learning
092   dynamics (Borras et al., 2022). We therefore claim neither generic novelty for training-dependent
093   fragility nor a smooth training law.
094
095
      Parameter, activation, and function space. Network composition, normalization, location, and
096   direction decouple raw perturbation size from functional change. Conversely, norm dominates recent
097   gradient-free language-model adaptation experiments in their specific regime (Kim et al., 2026). Our
098   result rejects only a pure raw-displacement account within the frozen attenuation assay. Forward
099   KL, NLL change, and top-1 damage share logits, so their alignment remains descriptive without
100   controlled contrasts.
101
102   Mechanistic and matched interventions. Activation patching and editing depend on corruption,
103   metric, and intervention design (Zhang & Nanda, 2023; Makelov et al., 2024); self-repair adds further
104   architecture- and prompt-specific dependence (Rushing & Nanda, 2024). SteerCheck is the closest
105   methodological collision: it prospectively matches off-target KL across steering comparators and
106   retains direction/family structure (Luo et al., 2026). We do not claim matched-KL auditing as new;
107   we target joint KL/NLL for training-conditioned block substitutability and add independent-run
      replication and explicit assay repair.

109   argmax changes under pruning and quantization. On identical teacher-forced positions, our S is one
110   minus such a disagreement rate; novelty is claimed only for the controlled result built around it.

      The family residual may still be a simple artifact of the top-1 decision rule. KL and NLL summarize

348   The baseline model conditions on the functional-damage summary and fixed run/checkpoint/layer
349   effects. Adding the intact top-1 margin alone does not change the family coefficient. Adding anchored
350
      boundary displacement also fails to shrink it: the estimated magnitude grows by 1.49%. A full block
      that adds logit-change norm and absolute alignment reduces the family coefficient by 20.18%, from
351
      approximately −0.0168 to −0.01344 (95% interval [−0.01764, −0.00923]). The point estimate

      remains outside the frozen equivalence region, although its interval overlaps the boundary slightly.

      8.1   I NTERVENTIONS AS CONDITIONAL MEASUREMENTS
366
367   Raw displacement is insufficient within residual attenuation, and functional damage is more informa-
368   tive, yet joint KL/NLL matching leaves a stable block/noise difference. Intervention identity therefore
369   retains information not captured by either severity description; it is not itself an explanation. A layer
370   may look substitutable under one procedure and not another because the procedures trace different
371   paths through activation space. Intervention claims should consequently report the model, data,
372   family, magnitude convention, functional-damage budget, response metric, and support diagnostics
373   rather than describe protocol-independent “layer redundancy.”
374
375   8.2   W HAT REMAINS UNIDENTIFIED
376
377   The two families induce strongly different hidden perturbation directions, but frozen observational
      direction features do not pass their preregistered explanatory gates. A planned counterfactual direction


                                                          7
378
      test fails its support gate before top-1 outcome reveal. We therefore make no causal claim about
379   internal direction; the blinded feasibility results remain in Appendix J.

381
      8.3   L IMITATIONS
382
383   Scope. Evidence is limited to 70M/160M P YTHIA replicas, two English evaluation streams with one
384   tokenizer, and two intervention families; cross-family damage matching is 160M and WikiText-2 only.
385   Corpus magnitudes differ by about 23%, and replacement, interchange, attention-only, MLP-only,
386   editing, other architectures, and billion-parameter models remain untested.
387
388   Construct validity and heterogeneity. S is discontinuous teacher-forced top-1 agreement, not
389   semantic equivalence, downstream capability, or pruning safety. KL, NLL, geometry, and DS share
390   logits, so matched associations may retain common metric geometry. Although the aggregate family
391   residual is seed-stable, 24/103 cells reverse sign and layer effects vary; low support at layer 7 limits
392   location-specific interpretation.
393
394   Mechanism and assay repair. The frozen tests weaken raw-magnitude, family-invariant damage,
395   and simple output-geometry accounts but do not identify an internal mechanism. The first prospective
396   reveal was invalidated by inconsistent FP16 top-1 tie handling; we stopped, withdrew it, froze a
397   family-independent lowest-index rule, and recomputed without changing matches or exclusions. The
398
      corrected conclusion survives, but the 12.49% point-estimate change is material; full provenance is in
      Appendix F.

401   9     C ONCLUSION
402
403   We tested whether protocol-dependent layer-intervention responses reduce to raw magnitude or
404   output-level predictive damage. Across independent small P YTHIA runs and two streams, functional
405   KL/NLL damage is more informative than raw displacement within attenuation, yet prospective
406   damage matching leaves a corrected block/noise residual that simple output geometry only partly
407   explains. The mechanism remains unidentified; under the tested conditions, intervention responses
408   are protocol-conditioned measurements rather than protocol-independent layer properties.
