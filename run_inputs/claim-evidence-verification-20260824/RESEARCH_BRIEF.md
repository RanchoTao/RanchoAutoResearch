# Independent overnight run: Claim-Evidence Verification for Autonomous AI Research

## Scope and authorization

This is a new independent run. Do not read from, write into, resume, or modify any other ResearchClaw run. The human has explicitly authorized autonomous progress through Stage 15, including bounded implementation and real pilot experiments. Routine literature and experiment-design gates are pre-approved for this run and must be logged. Stop before paper writing, publication, submission, external communication, purchases, deployment, or expensive scaling.

Hard limits:

- API/LLM budget approximately USD 5. The configured guardrail is USD 5; report if ACP cannot provide exact dollar accounting.
- At most 4 GPU-hours total on one RTX 5060 Laptop with about 8 GB VRAM.

- At most two genuine scientific pivots. After two failed pivots, decide KILL.
- Never fabricate literature, results, metrics, execution, or provenance.

## Research question

Study whether an automated verifier can determine if a scientific claim is supported by the literature, experiment design, artifacts, and observed results available to an autonomous research agent:

`(claim, literature, experiment design, artifacts, results) -> support status and failure reasons`.

Do not assume a fixed label space. Candidate support statuses are supported, partially supported, unsupported, contradicted, and insufficient/unverifiable. The stronger objective is diagnosis: novelty failure, missing baseline, confounding, leakage, weak statistics, cherry-picking, claim-evidence mismatch, missing robustness/causal isolation, unsupported generalization, compute unfairness, and reproducibility mismatch.

Treat this as an ML/agent-reliability problem, not a checklist-writing exercise. Optimize for whether an ICLR-level contribution genuinely exists, not for producing paper-shaped text.

## Literature requirements

Aggressively audit primary sources from 2023–2026 on autonomous science agents, AI Scientist systems, research-agent evaluation, scientific claim/evidence verification, scientific fact checking, automated peer review, experiment and reproducibility validation, scientific-reasoning benchmarks, LLM-as-judge reliability, verifier/self-critique/falsification, research-code benchmarks, and claim-evidence alignment.

Produce, if genuinely available:

- at least 30 relevant papers/systems;
- 10 highly relevant core works;
- 5 nearest neighbors.

For each nearest neighbor record exact task, input, output, use of experiment artifacts, novelty checking, falsification behavior, benchmark, metrics, unsolved gap, and threat level. Use verified titles/URLs/identifiers and explicit provenance.

## Failure-first analysis

Before proposing a method, determine which failures are common, automatically measurable, and insufficiently solved. Cover claim inflation, novelty hallucination, baseline omission, confounding, weak statistics, leakage, compute unfairness, selective evidence, failure to falsify, and artifact/report mismatch. Prefer evidence from real papers, peer-review or research-agent outputs where available.

## Benchmark investigation

First investigate reuse of existing claim-verification, scientific fact-checking, peer-review, research-agent trace, and experiment-validity datasets. If no dataset jointly contains claims, literature context, designs/artifacts, and outcomes, construct a compact traceable benchmark.

Permitted sources include public ML papers/traces and controlled synthetic or semi-synthetic mutations. Include valid cases and hard negatives generated by scientifically meaningful transformations: remove a decisive baseline, reduce seeds, inject train/test leakage, turn correlation into causation, broaden beyond environments, swap a nearest neighbor, hide contradictory results, change compute, or alter a key metric. Preserve realism and provenance; prevent trivial keyword or template detection; use held-out mutation/scenario families where possible.

## Candidate methods

Generate at most three candidates only after the audit. Generic frontier LLM judging is a baseline. Consider structured verification, adversarial falsification, evidence graphs, or critic/verifier debate only if the literature supports the need. Select at most one primary method for the pilot; avoid a complicated agent architecture unless structure is empirically justified.

## Pilot gate and minimum comparison

Run one bounded real pilot only if a credible gap survives. Target 100–500 cases if cheaply constructible. Compare exactly the necessary families:

1. strong generic frontier LLM judge available through current Codex ACP tooling;
2. structured checklist/prompt baseline;
3. strongest relevant existing verifier or the closest faithful retrieval-grounded implementation;
4. one proposed method.

Do not claim contribution from beating a weak zero-shot prompt. Use held-out templates/scenarios, documented class distribution, and multiple failure types. Primary risk-sensitive metric should heavily penalize declaring unsupported claims supported. Also report macro-F1, failure-type performance, calibration where scores exist, false-support and false-rejection rates, confidence intervals, runtime, request count, GPU time, and negative results. Match context/retrieval and model calls where scientifically possible, or state the mismatch.

## Reviewer simulation

Attack benchmark artificiality, circular LLM generation/judging, ground-truth ambiguity, contamination, judge bias, weak connection to real research, checklist triviality, insufficient technical depth, inability to detect novel scientific errors, domain dependence, low inter-annotator agreement, and overreliance on synthetic failures. For every attack state the evidence needed to survive.

## Final dossier and decision

Stop at Stage 15 with a concise dossier containing final research question, 30/10/5 literature map, exact gap, benchmark provenance, at most one primary method, executed pilot configuration/runtime/compute/results/uncertainty/negative results, baseline fidelity, top five rejection reasons, and 0–10 scores for novelty, importance, technical depth, benchmark quality, experimental strength, compute feasibility, and ICLR fit.

Return exactly one final decision: GO, PIVOT, or KILL. If GO, identify exactly one next experiment with highest expected information gain. If PIVOT, it must change the scientific question. If KILL, preserve reusable assets and name what killed the line.
